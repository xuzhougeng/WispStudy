# CI, releases and the project page

## CI and releases

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs clippy with `-D warnings` and the tests on Windows and macOS, and the three windowless crates on Linux. A release is a pushed tag `v<version>` that matches the version in `Cargo.toml` (`release.yml`; notes from `.github/release-notes/v<version>.md`). It is always signed, the way BiblioCell does it: `scripts/package-macos.sh --arch universal` builds, signs with the Developer ID certificate, notarizes and staples the app and the DMG; `scripts/package-windows.ps1 -Sign` signs both exes through SSL.com eSigner (`scripts/sign-windows-esigner.ps1`), zips their folder (the portable package) and compiles the setup from the same folder (`scripts/wisp-study.iss`, Inno Setup 6: for the current user, into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Wisp Study`, so the app can replace its own files there; its `AppId` stays `WispPaper`, the name before the rename, so it upgrades an old install in place and removes the old `WispPaper.exe` and shortcuts; the setup is signed, its uninstaller is not), and the workflow installs and removes it once without a question; Linux is an unsigned tar.gz. The ten secrets are listed at the top of `release.yml`; with one missing the run stops before building. A pull request touching `scripts/` or the workflow rehearses the packaging unsigned. Locally `CARGO="cargo +stable" scripts/package-macos.sh` gives an ad hoc signed `dist/Wisp Study.app`.

### A release, step by step

1. **What it brings.** `git log --first-parent v<previous>..origin/main` lists the pull requests merged since the last tag, and `git diff v<previous> origin/main -- docs/FEATURES.md` what they changed for a person, in the words the window uses. The notes are written from these.
2. **The release commit**: the version in the four `Cargo.toml` files and in `Cargo.lock` (the four `wisp_*` entries), and `.github/release-notes/v<version>.md` in the form of the one before it (what is new by subject with the pull request numbers, thanks to those who reported, 需要知道的 with what changed on disk and what was not tested). It goes in as a pull request; `cargo metadata --locked` must still read the manifests.
3. **The tag** `v<version>` on the merge commit, pushed once CI is green there. It starts the signed release, and the app of everyone who has one offers the update: it is not taken back, so the owner says go.
4. **After the tag: are tutorials called for?** With the tag pushed, go through what the release brought (the list of step 1) against `site/tutorials/` and tell the owner what you find: each new feature a person would use (a new way to read, ask, write or collect; not a fix, a default or a setting's wording) that no tutorial shows, and for each a suggestion, a tutorial of its own when it is a scenario by itself (reading several papers against each other, listening to a paper) or a section in the tutorial whose scenario it belongs to; or that nothing needs one. This is a suggestion, not a step the release waits for, and nothing is written until the owner says so. A tutorial is a record of a real run (see "The tutorials" below): a demo library, a model and an hour or more. The 版本 line of `site/tutorials/index.html` says up to which version the tutorials were written.

## `site/`: the project page

https://xuzhougeng.github.io/WispStudy/ is hand-written files: the front page (`index.html`, `style.css`, `site.js`) and the tutorials (`tutorials/*.html`, `tutorial.css`), no generator and nothing loaded from another host (no web fonts: the stacks are system ones). `site/build.sh [folder]` adds the screenshots from `docs/images` and the icon; `python3 -m http.server --directory dist/site` shows the result. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` deploys it on a push to main that touches it and after every published release, writing `release.json` (the latest release's files) next to the page: the download buttons read it, and lead to the release page without it. The page is laid out as a paper (numbered figures, citations that can be clicked); its numbers are those of `docs/TUTORIAL.md`, and its colours are `theme.rs`'s. Keep it out of `scripts/`, whose changes rehearse the packaging.

### The English pages

Every page has an English twin at the same path under `site/en/` (`site/en/index.html`, `site/en/tutorials/<slug>.html`), served as `…/WispStudy/en/…`. A twin has the same elements in the same order as the Chinese page and differs only in its text and in one more `../` on what it shares: the styles, `site.js` and the pictures are those of the Chinese pages, so the tutorials' screenshots show the Chinese interface, and each tutorial's colophon says so. The front page has screenshots of its own, of the English interface: `docs/images/en/library.png` and `docs/images/en/tutorial/*.jpg`, served as `img/en/…`. They were taken with `shot:` at `size:1372;870` after `language:en`, against a `WISP_STUDY_HOME` of their own and a stand-in model (a script speaking the Claude stream as `mock_model` does, with English answers worked out by rules from each request), which §1 of the English page says; a dialog's text is typed with untimed `key:` steps, which arrive in order where timed ones do not. The top bar of every page carries a link to its twin (`.lang`), and the head two `hreflang` lines. `site.js` writes its words from the page's `lang` (the `SAY` table) and finds `release.json` beside itself; a rule that only holds for one language is a `:lang(en)` rule in the style sheets. Labels of the window are written as the English interface shows them: the value of the Chinese label in `locales/en.json`, in “ ”. A change to a Chinese page is made to its twin too, and a new tutorial is two pages.

### The tutorials

`site/tutorials/` holds one page per scenario and `index.html`, their list; the front page leads to them from the end of §1 (`.guides`) and its top bar. A tutorial is a record of a real run on the demo topic (the library 「植物单细胞转录组」), told as numbered sections with screenshots. A new one is a copy of `site/tutorials/_template.html`, which shows every element a tutorial may use (`tutorial.css` styles them from `style.css`'s tokens; a page carries no styles or scripts of its own, and `build.sh` leaves files starting with `_` out of the site). Its pictures are `docs/images/tutorials/<slug>-NN-<name>.jpg`, served as `img/t/…`: taken with the `shot:` step of `WISP_STUDY_DEV` (see `debugging.md`) against a `WISP_STUDY_HOME` of its own and scaled with `sips -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 72 -Z 1372`. The top bar, the footer and the pager are repeated in every page: a change to one is a change to all of them, and a new tutorial also needs its entry in `tutorials/index.html`, in the front page's `.guides`, and in the pager of its neighbours. What a tutorial says about the window has to be checked against the window: labels are quoted in 「」 as displayed.
