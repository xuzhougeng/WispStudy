Tutorial 07 · Read

Read several papers side by side

Two papers study the same thing with different methods, and when you read one and then open the other, you forget what the first one said. This tutorial puts two single-cell studies of trichome development side by side: each with its own translation, asked about together with the sources opened in their own panes, one of them read in a separate window, going back and forward between them, and finally finding the conversation again in History.

Scenario
Compare two studies of trichome development: one uses protoplasts for single-cell sequencing, the other uses nuclei.
Needs
Two or three papers with PDFs, and a model provider set up in “Settings › AI”.
Time
About 15 minutes, not counting the reading itself.

1Pick the papers to compare

Type trichome in the search box above the paper list. Three papers are left in the library “植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics), all about trichomes:

  • Single-cell transcriptome of Nepeta tenuifolia leaves reveal differentiation trajectories in glandular trichomes (Zhou et al., 2022, Frontiers in Plant Science, 15 pages). Young leaves of Nepeta tenuifolia are digested into protoplasts for single-cell scRNA-seq. It studies the peltate glandular trichomes that secrete essential oil.
  • Comprehensive insights into the development of arabidopsis trichomes by single-nucleus RNA-seq (Chen et al., 2026, BMC Plant Biology, 16 pages). It reanalyzes public single-nucleus snRNA-seq data from Arabidopsis and studies the unicellular, branched, non-secreting trichomes.
  • The paper on tea plant trichomes shows “Linked” in its Full text column. The library holds no PDF for it, so it is not used this time.

The first two ask the same question: along what trajectory do trichome cells differentiate from the epidermis? Their methods are opposite: one first digests the leaf into protoplasts, the other takes only nuclei. What you most want to know while reading is what each method sees and whether the conclusions can be stated together. That is what side-by-side reading is for.

2Keep one beside

Click the Nepeta paper first and switch to “PDF”. The detail panel on the right is narrow, so the PDF toolbar shows only icons. Press ⌘ + Shift + F (Ctrl + Shift + F on Windows) to enter reading mode, or click the expand icon to the right of the title at the top of the detail. The toolbar is now wide enough to show the names of its buttons, including “Keep beside” and “New window”.

Click “Keep beside”. The window returns to the paper list, the pushpin lights up, and a hint at the top says “This paper is kept. Choose another in the list and the two stand side by side.” Now click the Arabidopsis paper in the list and the two fill the window side by side: the Nepeta pane on the left, the Arabidopsis pane on the right. Another way is to right-click a paper in the list and choose “Open in a pane beside” from the menu. According to the feature description, up to 3 papers can be kept beside; this example puts only two side by side.

What is kept is the paper that is open, so once you have kept it, pick the next paper directly in the list. If you press Esc to close it first, the mark is gone and you have to keep it again.

Each pane beside has only one line on top: which paper, which page, and a × at the right end to close the pane. This line is the same height in every pane, so the pages start from the same line. The toolbar of the current pane is also trimmed down: page number, zoom, “Translation”, the pushpin, and the other buttons are under ⋯. While the papers are side by side, the top right corner of the window shows the restore icon, whose hint reads “Leave reading mode Esc”. Click it or press Esc to leave.

3The current pane: it changes only when you click

One of the panes is always the “current pane”: it has a green line on top, and the PDF toolbar, the shortcuts, the title at the top of the window and “Info” all follow it. Click once in the Nepeta pane on the left, and the green line and the toolbar move to the left, and the title at the top changes to the Nepeta paper. The Arabidopsis pane shrinks to one line, and the page numbers and zoom of every pane stay as they were.

Scrolling another pane to take a look does not change the current pane. In Figure 3, the current pane is the Nepeta paper on the left, and the Arabidopsis paper on the right has been scrolled to Figure 1 on page 3. The page number at the top of that pane changes to “3 / 16 pages”, while the green line and the toolbar stay on the left.

4Layout: choose how to read in one step

The drop-down button to the right of “Info”, “PDF” and “Ask” at the top of the window is “Layout”. While papers are side by side, the button shows the one in use. Opening it gives four: “Reading” shows only the original; “With translation” gives each paper its own translation; “Ask” puts the conversation on one side and the original on the other; “Close reading” fills the window, with the original and the translation one above the other and the conversation on the right. When several papers are side by side, each gets an equal share, and the menu does not state a column ratio.

With translation

Choose “With translation”. Each pane is split into two halves, the original on top and the translation of the same page below; in this example the window is 1400 pixels wide, too narrow for four panes in a row. Only one paper is translated at a time: the Arabidopsis paper, the current pane, starts first, and its toolbar shows the progress and “Stop”. In the line of the Nepeta pane beside it, “Continue translating” appears; click it and its progress is shown in that line too. The translation model in this example is codex/gpt-6-luna. After two and a half minutes the Arabidopsis paper had translated 103 of 137 paragraphs, and the Nepeta paper took under three minutes for its 111 paragraphs. Translated paragraphs are saved in the library and shown directly the next time you open the paper.

Close reading

After both papers are translated, switch to “Close reading”: the two panes on the left each show the original on top and the translation below, and the conversation is on the right. While the conversation is still empty, it says “Ask about these papers” at the top, with three comparison questions listed below: “How do the research questions and methods of these papers differ?”, “Do their conclusions agree? Where do they support each other, and where do they differ?”, “Compare these papers in a table: subject, methods, data and main findings.” Click one to send it.

The Nepeta paper opens right on page 12 because that is where you read to last time (see section 7).

5Ask about several papers together

Switch to the “Ask” layout. The conversation is on the left, the two originals are on the right, and there is no paper list. Type in the box:

Ask

Both papers study trichome development: what material, sampling method and sequencing method did each use (single-cell or single-nucleus)? Which marker genes were used to identify trichome cells? How are their conclusions about the developmental trajectory alike and different? Give the source for each paper.

Above the conversation it says “Provided the records of these 2 papers and …”, which means the records and full texts of the two papers are sent to the model provider you set up (here claude/claude-sonnet-5-5). The answer is done after a little over a minute. In the answer the two papers are numbered P12 (Nepeta) and P26 (Arabidopsis). It first separates material, sampling and sequencing in a table: P12 uses young leaves of 25-day-old seedlings, digested into protoplasts, with 10x Genomics single-cell sequencing, yielding 33,254 cells; P26 did no sampling of its own and reanalyzes public snRNA-seq data, so it needs no protoplasts. Then it lists the marker genes of each paper separately: P12 relies on annotation by Arabidopsis homologs, and P26 uses GL1, GL2 and MYB106 to divide trichomes into initiation, intermediate and branching stages.

The section on the similarities and differences in trajectory is the most useful. What they share is that both papers pick out the epidermal and trichome cells, recluster them, and then use Monocle for pseudotime. What differs is that the P12 trajectory forks from the epidermis into glandular trichomes and another type of trichome, while P26 has one staged trajectory. It also points out the one thing the two papers most need to be read together on: the glandular trichomes in P12 number only 45 cells, the authors admit trichomes are hard to protoplast, and they suggest snRNA-seq may be the solution, which is exactly what P26 uses, nuclei. Finally it cautions that the trichome type of undetermined identity in P12 “should not be mapped directly onto the branching stage of P26”.

Below the answer are the source cards. Each shows which paper, which page and one sentence of the original, and a checked one has “Exact quote found” at its right end. Click the “P12 p.12” card: the Nepeta pane becomes the current pane, jumps to page 12 and highlights the sentence “Young leaves were harvested from 25-d-old seedlings” in yellow. Then click the “P26 p.2” card: the Arabidopsis pane jumps to page 2 and marks the sentence where it reanalyzes public data, while the Nepeta pane stays on page 12. Each paper is checked in its own pane, with no flipping back and forth.

Whichever pane you read in, it is the same conversation, and in History it is stored under these two papers together. Adding or removing a pane starts a new conversation.

Note“Exact quote found” only shows that the quoted sentence is in the original, not that the comparison in the answer is right. In this example some statements in the answer carry no quoted sentence, for instance that P12 used 3 biological replicates; turn to that page yourself to confirm it before you state it.

6Open a separate window

If you want to read the full text of the Nepeta paper while looking at other papers in the library, click “New window” on the PDF toolbar. The paper moves into a separate window, and the library window returns to the list. Click the Arabidopsis paper in the list and its “Info” opens on the right as usual. The two do not disturb each other.

The top of the reading window shows the title, page number and zoom, and “Library” opens this paper in the library window. The toolbar here has no “Translation”: translation cannot be turned on in the reading window for now. To see a translation, go back to the library window.

7Back, forward, and staying where you were

You can also go back and forth when the papers are not side by side. Read the Nepeta paper in the detail panel and turn to page 3, then switch to the Arabidopsis paper in the list. Press ⌘ + [ (Ctrl + [ on Windows) to go back: you return to the Nepeta paper and the PDF still stands at “3 / 15 pages”. Press ⌘ + ] to go forward and you are back on page 1 of the Arabidopsis paper.

It is not only back and forward: every paper opens again where you last left it. Clicking a source card in section 5 jumped to page 12 of the Nepeta paper, and after that, whether you open it alone or side by side again, it starts from page 12.

8Find the conversation again in History

The next time you put the two papers side by side and switch to “Ask”, the conversation is empty. Click “History” above the conversation. The title is “Past conversations about these papers”, the switch above reads “These papers”, and below it the conversations are grouped by day. The comparison is under “Today”, with the time and “1 questions”. Click that line and the answer and the source cards come back as they were, and you can keep asking.

With only one paper open, the switch reads “This paper”, with “All” beside it. “All” lists the library's conversations with the assistant and with each paper, and under each line it says which paper it belongs to (or “Assistant”). Conversations held with several papers side by side are not among them; to find those, open “History” while the papers are side by side. The search box searches title, paper and what was said: type “毛状体” (trichome) and only one question about the Nepeta paper and one to the assistant remain, and the /slides conversation about callus is gone.

A conversation uses its first question as its title by default. When that is too long to find, use “Rename…” on the line. The “Rename conversation” dialog opens with the old title in the box; change it and click “Rename”. Under the box it says “Leave it empty to use the first question as the title.”

Recap

  • Put several papers side by side with “Keep beside” or with right-click “Open in a pane beside”. A pane beside has a single line; click × to close it.
  • The current pane changes only when you click; scrolling another pane does not change it. The green line, the toolbar and “Info” follow the current pane.
  • “Layout” chooses how to read in one step: “With translation” gives each paper its translation, and “Ask” and “Close reading” put the conversation beside the papers.
  • A question asked while papers are side by side is asked about those papers together, source cards jump to that paper's own pane, and each pane stays where it was.
  • “New window” opens one paper alone, and “Back” and “Forward” move between the papers you have read. This conversation is under “These papers” in “History”; conversations about a single paper and with the assistant are under “All”.
