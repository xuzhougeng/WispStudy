Tutorial 09 · Read

Ask the whole library, and check the answers

You meet your advisor tomorrow and want a feel for the whole field first: which species and organs are covered, what the protoplast and nucleus routes each cost, and where the papers disagree. In this tutorial the assistant reads the whole library to answer, and then you check the passages it cites one by one instead of taking its word.

Scenario
Before a lab meeting, an overview of the 28 papers in the “植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics) library.
Needs
A screened library in which most papers have a stored PDF; a model provider.
Time
About 10 minutes, most of it waiting for the answer.

Contents

  1. Set the scope first
  2. Ask, and see what it read
  3. Read the sources and the check marks
  4. Go back to the page of the original
  5. Have the assistant check each claim
  6. Compare, search and keep asking
  7. Recap

1Set the scope first

Click “Assistant” on the left. Before a conversation starts, the page lists a few kinds of things it can do: “Find papers”, “Read papers”, “Organise” and “Write”. Under each group are a few ready-made sentences and slash commands; click one to send it. The line under the title is the scope: “Scope: 28 papers in the current list”.

The scope is the list currently shown in “All papers”. Search or filter there first, and the assistant reads only that part. For example, search for nuclei first and the scope becomes “Scope: 5 papers in the current list”. You can also click “Papers” at the top right of the Assistant page and type more words in the panel that opens to narrow it down: look for protoplast among those 5 papers and the scope becomes “Scope: 1 papers matching “protoplast””. Each row of the panel has a plus sign on the right; click it (or drag the row into the conversation) to read only that one paper, and “The first question reads only these 1” appears above the question box.

Here you want an overview of the whole library, so do not filter: use all 28 papers.

2Ask, and see what it read

Write the question in the question box and press Enter to send it (Shift + Enter for a new line):

Ask

Which species and organs does the research in this library cover? What does each of the two routes, protoplasts (scRNA-seq) and nuclei (snRNA-seq), cost? Where do the papers disagree? Please quote page numbers and original sentences from the full text wherever you can.

After you send it, the scope line changes to “Provided records for 28 papers, 40 relevant full-text pages”. When there are too many papers to fit, the rest get only their titles and this line says “N titles only”; this library is small, so all 28 papers were given as full records. Click “Papers” again and the panel now lists the materials handed to the model this time: each paper has a number P1, P2 and so on, then “Full record” or “Full record · 5 full-text pages”, and whether the paper has a full text.

This panel is worth a look: 7 papers, including P1, P4, P6, P7 and P8, are marked “No full text”, so anything said about them can only come from the abstract. The papers that do have a full text were sent only the pages relevant to the question, and when the assistant thinks that is not enough it searches and pages through them itself.

This time the assistant called no tools and finished writing in about two minutes: first a table of species, organs and study routes, then sections on the cost of each route and on where the papers disagree. The opening paragraph is:

Assistant

The experimental subjects in this library are mainly Arabidopsis, rice and maize, and have extended to sweet potato, Nepeta, tea plant, Chinese cabbage, patchouli and reed; the organs covered are root, leaf, shoot apex, seed, node and regenerating material.

3Read the sources and the check marks

Sources in an answer are written in two ways. [P13] says only which paper the claim rests on; [P11 p.9 "The composition of the cell wall…"] also gives the PDF page number and the sentence word for word. Below the answer there is one card for each source: the first 8 are shown, and the rest are kept under “41 more sources”, visible only when you open it.

A card with a quoted sentence ends with a check result: the app compares the sentence letter by letter with the text saved for that paper (only letters and digits count; line breaks and hyphens do not). The possible results are:

  • “Exact quote found”: the sentence is on the page named (or in the abstract).
  • “Quote found on p.N”: the words are in the paper, but on another page; clicking the card goes to the page where they really are.
  • “Only the first N% matches”: the beginning matches, and the rest was changed or spliced together.
  • “Quote absent from source”: it cannot be found anywhere in the full text.
  • “No full text to verify”: the paper has no readable full text, and the sentence is not in the abstract either.

This time all 21 quoted sentences read “Exact quote found”, and no other result appeared. But that only shows that “the sentence is in the original”, not that it supports the conclusion before it. Step 5 shows that this same answer, whose quotes all match, still overstates a conclusion in places.

4Go back to the page of the original

Click a card with a quoted sentence, for example “P12 p.2 · However, this approach is problematic…”. The PDF of that paper opens on the right, turned to page 2, with the sentence painted yellow.

In P12 this sentence is the introduction summing up earlier methods; the paper did not measure it itself. The answer uses it as evidence for “the cost of the protoplast route”. That is fine, but it weighs differently from an experiment that makes a direct comparison. A matching program cannot see that difference; you have to read the context.

5Have the assistant check each claim

In the same conversation, type /verify and send it; with nothing else written, it checks the previous answer. It asks the assistant to list the claims one by one, find the source of each in the full text, read the context of that page, and write “claim → excerpt from the original → how far it is supported”. Where only the abstract is available, it says so and gives no page number.

This run took about 5 minutes. The conversation first shows two gray rows, “Search full text ×25 · Read full text ×10” and “Read full text ×20 · Get paper ×7 · Search full text”. Click one to expand it: one row per call, saying which words were searched, which paper was read and from which page how many pages, and a result such as “11 results” or “read 2 pages”.

The result lists 99 claims, and its 66 excerpts from the originals all read “Exact quote found” as well. What is worth reading is how it corrects the previous answer:

Assistant

P22 itself uses the wording “broadly comparable”, so it is not in direct contradiction with P23; the range of species also needs to count the cotton data that P25 actually reanalyzed.

It also points out that the PDF stored for P9 is supplementary material, so its page numbers cannot be taken as page numbers of the main text, and that P1, P4, P7, P14 and P18 can only be checked against their abstracts, so those claims have no page numbers.

To keep asking about one sentence, drag across it; when you let go, “Quote and ask” and “Copy” appear next to the selection. Click “Quote and ask” and the sentence goes into the question box as a quoted block; write your question after it and send.

TipThe verdicts of /verify come from the model too. Where it says “directly supported”, still open the original and look, above all for numbers that will go into lab-meeting slides.

6Compare, search and keep asking

Click “New chat” to start another question. Two slash commands come in handy when preparing for a lab meeting:

  • /compare P22 P12: it first reads the records and full texts of the two papers (this time P22 pages 1 to 14 and P12 pages 1 to 13), compares research question, materials and methods, conclusions and limitations in a table, then says where they agree, complement each other or contradict each other. This time it concluded that neither paper directly refutes the other, and pointed out that P12's own statements about how ASE changes with development are inconsistent. The numbers depend on the scope: with the same 28 papers, P22 and P12 are the two in the panel.
  • /search ambient RNA: find a phrase in the stored full texts and records. This time it listed 4 papers, each with a page number and the original sentence, and said that 7 more papers have only a full-text link and were checked by record only, so nothing can be concluded about whether their text mentions it.

Every conversation is saved in this library. Click “History” at the top right to list past conversations and how many questions each had; open one and under the title it says “Past conversation: continuing will provide materials from the library's current content”, and you can keep asking.

When the assistant wants to change the library, it stops and asks you first. This time it was asked to “find open full text for papers that have no PDF yet”, and a card appeared in the conversation: “Assistant requests permission to change this library”, with the operation to be done, “Find open full text: limit 50”, and below it “Allow”, “Allow for this conversation” and “Deny”.

Questions you ask often can be saved as your own commands: in “Settings › AI › Commands”, click “Add command” and fill in a name, a description and a template; where the template has {}, whatever follows the command is put in. After that, typing / in the question box lets you pick it. A command is only a piece of prompt: it gives the assistant no extra tools and skips no confirmation.

NoteWhat is sent to the model provider you set up in settings when you ask is the question itself, the records and notes of the papers in scope, the relevant full-text pages, and whatever the assistant reads with its tools. For papers with no stored PDF the assistant can read only the abstract; the answer says so and gives no page number. For the few papers you will present at the lab meeting, drag their PDFs into the window to attach them, then ask again.

Recap

  • The scope is the list currently shown in “All papers”; you can search first, or narrow it or read just one paper in the “Papers” panel.
  • After you ask, the “Provided…” line and the “Papers” panel show what was handed to the model this time.
  • Sources with a page number and a quoted sentence are compared word for word, the result is written on the card, and clicking the card goes back to that page of the PDF.
  • “Exact quote found” says only that the words are in the original; whether the conclusion holds is for /verify and your own reading of the context to decide.
  • To read the whole library into one table, paper by paper, continue with Read a batch of papers into one table; for a deep read of one paper, go back to Read one paper closely.

About this tutorial. The screenshots and numbers come from a real run in November 2026 on macOS with v0.10.0. The model answers differently each time, so your wording and numbers will differ; the steps are the same. The screenshots show the Chinese interface; the text uses the labels of the English one (Settings › General › Interface language).

The assistant can be wrong. Confirm its suggestions and answers yourself; check key numbers and conclusions against the original.
