Tutorial 01 · Collect

Start from a topic: search, import, screen

Your advisor gave you a direction, plant single-cell transcriptomics, and you have no papers. This tutorial starts from an empty Wisp Study: set up a model, create a library, have the assistant draft search queries, import 968 papers from PubMed and Europe PMC, let the assistant read each abstract and make a suggestion, and finally decide for yourself what to include.

Scenario
You have just taken over a research topic and want a first batch of papers in one afternoon, sorted into those worth reading and those not.
Needs
Wisp Study and a model provider of your own (Claude API, OpenAI-compatible or OpenAI Responses endpoint).
Time
Little of your own time; most of it is waiting for the model and the databases.

1Install, set up a model

Download from the release page: on macOS it is a .dmg, and after opening it you drag Wisp Study into the Applications folder. Windows has an installer, -setup.exe (it installs for the current user and needs no administrator rights), and a portable .zip. Drafting queries and screening both call a model. Wisp Study does not ship one, so first connect your own in Settings.

Click “Settings”, the gear that is last in the row of icons at the top right of the home page (⌘ + ,, or Ctrl + , on Windows) and go to “AI”. The “Models” tab lists the model providers on the left; here they are “Claude” and “DeepSeek”. A provider that has no API key yet is marked “No API key”: open it and fill in the key. For another provider, click “Add provider” and choose from the list, or choose “Other OpenAI-compatible service”:

  • Protocol: one of Claude API, OpenAI compatible and Responses. Any of the three works.
  • Base URL: for an OpenAI-compatible provider, write it up to /v1.
  • API Key: leave it empty to read the matching environment variable, for example OPENAI_API_KEY.
  • Model: in the empty box under “Model”, enter the model name the provider requires and click “Test” on the right. The app sends the model a very short message. If it gets through, the name gets a tick with the seconds it took, and the button turns into “Add”: click it and the model is added. If not, it is marked “Failed” with the reason under it, most often a wrong name, base URL or key; put it right and test again. The menu to the left of “Test” is the kind of model, recognised from the name by default (chat, image or judging model); here both are chat models. An image model is not tested and can be added straight away.

Once added, a model's name cannot be changed: if it is wrong, delete it with the × at the end of its row and add it again. One provider can hold several models. Each model has its own thinking level and its own “Test” at the end of its row, to try it again after you change the key, the base URL or the thinking level. There is no save button; changes are saved as you make them. When “No API key” under the provider's name is replaced by the number of models, it is ready to use.

One model is enough to get through the whole tutorial. Two more things were done this time. Under “Tasks”, “Assistant” uses claude-opus-5-5, while “Batch screening” and “Translate” use the smaller claude-haiku-5-5. And “Concurrent papers” was raised from the default 3 to 8, so that screening sends that many requests at once; lower it if the provider limits your rate.

NoteThe API key is saved in plain text in settings.json on your machine. When a model is used, the topic, your search requirements and the abstracts of papers are sent to the model provider you configured.

2Create a library, write the topic

Go back to the home page and click “New library” (⌘ + ⇧ + N). Name it “Plant single-cell transcriptomics”. The topic is more than a note: the assistant reads it when it drafts queries, judges what to include and answers questions, so it is worth being specific. This time it says:

Topic

Single-cell and single-nucleus transcriptomics of plants (scRNA-seq / snRNA-seq): cell atlases of each species and organ; developmental trajectories and cell fate; responses to environmental stress; and methods such as protoplasting versus nuclei isolation; cell type annotation; and cross-species integration.

After you click “Create”, the page asks where to start. If your papers are already in Zotero or in an exported file, choose “Import existing papers” or “Import from Zotero”; see Tutorial 03. Here you have nothing, so choose the recommended “Start with a database search”.

3Have the assistant draft search queries

The topic is already filled into the input box, and you can edit it. Below it is a row of databases. PubMed is selected by default; also select Europe PMC, which holds preprints, and new work in this field often appears first on bioRxiv. Crossref and arXiv do not help much with this topic, so leave them out.

Click “Suggest queries”. After a short wait the assistant has drafted three queries with different orientations for each database: a broad one, one focused on atlases, development and stress, and one from the angle of methods such as sample preparation and annotation. Under each query is the result of a trial search run at once: how many papers the database matched in all, and what the newest few are.

The match counts of this trial search (the counts of the two methods queries were not recorded):

Look at the titles, not at how elegant the query is. A query that is too broad pulls in unrelated papers, as Figure 5 shows. A trial search writes nothing into the library. If you are not satisfied, edit the topic and click “Suggest again”, or click “Write a query yourself”. This time the two focused queries were chosen, the atlases, development and stress query for PubMed and the same one for Europe PMC. Click “Select”, and the button turns into “Selected”.

4Save and import

Click “Save and import 2 selected queries” at the bottom right. The two queries are saved to the “Search and import” page and run at once:

  • PubMed: 550 matched, 550 added.
  • Europe PMC: 914 matched, 418 added, 496 already held and skipped. The two databases overlap, and Wisp Study removes duplicates by DOI, PMID and title.
TipQueries saved by the guide retrieve up to the latest 2000 papers each time (200 for Crossref), asking the database for 200 at a time over several requests. Both queries here matched fewer than 2000 papers, so all of them came back. Change the limit under “Retrieve” of the query.

That makes 968 papers, all “Pending”: import a little wide first, then judge paper by paper. If you imported the wrong thing, “Undo” on the row in “Import history” removes that batch of newly added papers that nobody has handled yet. “Automatic updates” at the top of the page lets a query run by itself every 6 hours, daily or weekly, and new papers come in as “Pending” in the same way.

5Have the assistant screen

The list already shows some unrelated papers mixed in, for example one about the bioenergetic state of Bacillus subtilis. Reading 968 abstracts one by one would take far too long, so first let the assistant read them.

Open “Screening” on the left. The page head offers two views, “Batch” and “One by one”; use “Batch” first. Under “Screening criteria”, write what kind of paper belongs in this library. If you leave it empty, papers are judged by the topic alone, and a clearly drawn boundary is much more accurate:

Screening criteria

Include: original research, methods and data resources that study plants and actually produce or analyze single-cell or single-nucleus transcriptome data (scRNA-seq, snRNA-seq), and reviews of the field. Exclude: studies not on plants, studies that use only bulk RNA-seq, and papers that mention single-cell techniques only in passing.

Click “Ask assistant to screen 968 papers”. The assistant reads 8 papers at a time, and reads the full text where there is one; the suggestions in the figures here were made from the abstract alone. Under the progress bar are the papers being read, and those it has finished join the queue with their suggestions.

This round of 968 papers took about two minutes with the stand-in model used for this run, and the suggestions fell into four groups:

You can click “Stop” midway, and the papers not yet read wait for the next round. A paper that could not be judged does not interrupt the round. You can change the screening criteria at any time: the existing suggestions are marked as given under the old criteria, you have the assistant judge again under the current ones, and the decisions you already made are left alone.

6Confirm the screening decisions

The assistant's suggestions are only suggestions. It changes no paper's screening decision; only you do. When a round is finished, “The assistant has finished. Review its suggestions” appears under the progress bar, and the button at the top right becomes “Confirm N suggestions”. Click it to enter “Confirm suggestions”:

Each row is one paper, the assistant's reason and the decision it will get, all checked by default. If you disagree, click another decision on the right. If you are unsure, uncheck it and it stays pending. “Lowest confidence first” puts the ones that most need a look at the top. In Figure 8 the top two papers have no abstract or saved full text, so the assistant could only say “Insufficient evidence” and they stay unchecked. Further down, a title about Kinetoplastida parasites carries the suggestion “Related”; if you disagree, click “Irrelevant”. Finally click “Confirm N papers”, and the checked ones are written in one step as screening decisions, with the assistant's reasons recorded as the reasons for the decisions. Nothing changes before you write, and afterward “Undo” is available at the top of the page.

If you only want to handle one group first, click a group above the queue in “Batch”, for example “Irrelevant 32”, and the button becomes “Confirm 32 “Irrelevant” suggestions”. To look closely paper by paper, switch to “One by one” in the page head: one paper at a time, with the assistant's suggestion, reason and the sentence it relied on at the top (the sentence is quoted word for word from the abstract or full text, and a reason that cannot quote the original is not taken as evidence), the abstract in the middle and the four decisions at the bottom. Press Y for Included, R for Related, U for Insufficient evidence, N for Irrelevant and A to accept the suggestion, and it moves on to the next paper by itself.

That run ended up writing the 515 Included and the 32 Irrelevant as suggested, and one more paper was marked Irrelevant by hand. That left 420 pending (418 Related and 2 Insufficient evidence) for a look of your own. Back in “All papers”, the irrelevant ones are hidden by default, so the list shows 935 papers, and the “Screening” column has results.

Recap

  • In “Settings › AI” you connected your own model provider and raised the screening concurrency to 8.
  • You wrote a specific topic when creating the library, and from it the assistant drafted 6 queries for two databases. You chose 2 by the titles the trial search matched and imported 968 papers.
  • You wrote the screening criteria, and the assistant read 968 abstracts. You confirmed the Included and Irrelevant groups, and 935 papers stay in the library.
  • Next: read one of them closely, see Tutorial 05; ask questions of the whole library and check the sources, see Tutorial 10; write a review in stages with /review, see Tutorial 13.

About this tutorial. The screenshots were retaken on November 24, 2026 on macOS with v0.17.0, with the English interface and a stand-in model that answers by fixed rules, so the numbers in this tutorial are those of that run. Figure 1 was retaken on November 27, 2026 with v0.21.0, where a model is tested before it is added, against the same kind of stand-in model. A real model answers differently each time and the databases are updated every day, so the wording and numbers you see will differ a little. The steps are the same.

The assistant can be wrong. Its suggestions and answers need your confirmation; check key numbers and conclusions against the original.
