Tutorial 18 · Follow up

Several topics at once: work in the background, ask across libraries

You have more than one topic on the go: plant single-cell transcriptomics and root hair development, each with a library of its own. In this tutorial one library's assistant screens its papers in the background while you work in the other, and it is done when you come back. Then, on the home page, you ask the assistant a question that spans both libraries: it looks in both, every source of its answer says which library it comes from, and a click takes you to that paper in that library. Last, you copy a paper both topics can use into the other library, and choose between appending and overwriting where that library already has the same paper.

Scenario
“植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics) has 40 papers waiting to be screened, “根毛发育” (root hair development) 30 papers just brought in from PubMed; do the two meet anywhere on root hairs? A root atlas on the single-cell side would serve the root hair side too.
Needs
Two libraries, and a model provider set up in “Settings › AI”.
Time
About 15 minutes, of which screening 40 papers took a little over a minute in the background.

1Start screening in one library

The home page holds two libraries: “植物单细胞转录组”, 40 papers all still pending, and “根毛发育”, 30 papers just fetched with a PubMed search. Open the first one, click “Screening” in the sidebar and write the criteria under “Screening criteria”: only original research that sequences single cells or single nuclei in plants; reviews and method-only papers are not included. Then click “Ask assistant to screen 40 papers”.

The assistant starts reading paper by paper. The box says “Assistant reading 3 / 40”, and a paper it has read gets a suggestion at the right of the list, such as “Suggestion: Included · Confidence 99%”. How screening works and how suggestions are confirmed is in Start from a topic; here the model is GPT-6.1 Sol through a local gateway, reading three papers at a time.

2Leave it: the home page's card says how far it is

No need to wait. Click “← All libraries” at the top left to go home: the card of “植物单细胞转录组” now has a spinner and “Screening 7 / 40” at its bottom right. It goes on reading in the background and has reached the seventh paper.

Until this version, leaving a library stopped a batch like this, and it had to be started again. Now screening, extracting fields, finding open full texts and translating go on in the background, and their results go into their own library as before.

3Work on in another library

From the home page, open “根毛发育” and look through the 30 papers just fetched. At the bottom of this library's sidebar there is a line of progress: a spinner, “Screening · 植物…” and “19/40”, with a small arrow. Click that line to go back to the screening page of the other library; that is also where you stop it.

4Done: a line at the top of the window

Still browsing “根毛发育”, a little over a minute later a line appears at the top of the window: “Screening in “植物单细胞转录组” is done: 40”, and the progress line at the bottom of the sidebar is gone.

Back in the first library, all 40 papers have a suggestion: Included 23, Related 11, Irrelevant 5, Insufficient evidence 1. They are only suggestions: the papers are still “Pending” until you look them over under “Confirm”, exactly as if you had waited in the library for the assistant to finish.

TipWhat goes on in the background is screening, extracting, finding open full texts and translating, and a conversation still answering when you leave finishes its answer; a step that needs your agreement (an import, say) waits for you, and the card says “Waiting for you”. Importing PDFs, migrating from Zotero, text recognition and reading aloud stop when you leave the library (these are from the feature list; this tutorial did not try each). Quitting the app ends every task in the background, and what they had already found is kept.

5Back home, ask both libraries

With the screening done, you want to know whether the two topics meet on root hairs. The assistant inside a library keeps to that library; a question across libraries is asked on the home page. Click the assistant button at the bottom right of the home page: under the box is a row of starts, and “Ask my libraries” puts “Look this up in my libraries:” into the box.

Then write the question; here it was typed over the box's words: “Search my libraries: what do the two libraries 植物单细胞转录组 and 根毛发育 hold on root hairs (or root epidermal cell fate)? Back each point with a quoted sentence. Answer in English.” The model is Claude Sonnet 5.5 through a local gateway. Asked the same without “Answer in English.”, it answered in Chinese, though the question and the interface were English, so the last sentence was added for this run. It listed the papers of both libraries, searched their full texts for “root hair”, read the records of a few, and had answered within a minute and a half.

6Sources that name their library

The answer takes the two libraries one by one. Each point quotes a sentence, followed by a blue label such as [P24] or [P25]; for what it knew from titles only, it says so (“Microbes and stress are covered by titles only”). Its gap: “根毛发育” holds genetics but no single-cell root hair study, and “植物单细胞转录组” no paper on root hairs.

The source cards under the answer name the library of each paper, such as “P2 (根毛发育) · Glucose-TOR Signaling Regul…”, and those that do not fit are under “12 more sources”.

Click the card “P2 (根毛发育)”: the window opens the root hair library with this paper's details on the right, Glucose-TOR Signaling Regulates Root Hair Elongation in Arabidopsis via the RHD6-RSL4 Transcriptional Cascade. The conversation stays under “Recent conversations” on the home page.

NoteIn a conversation of the home page, the other libraries are read-only: the assistant can list papers, search and read full texts and read marks, but not add tags, change a status or import papers; open that library to change it. The “Assistant” inside a library still keeps to that library.

7One paper for both: copy it over

The answer in the last section said that “根毛发育” holds no single-cell study. That gap can be filled from the other library. “植物单细胞转录组” has a single-nucleus atlas of Tartary buckwheat roots (Hou et al., 2025, International journal of biological macromolecules), whose abstract says it maps the developmental trajectories of root hair and cortex cells. The single-cell topic still needs it, so it should not be moved; copy it, and both topics have it.

In “植物单细胞转录组”, type buckwheat into the search box above the list, and this paper is the only one left. Right-click it: in the last group of the menu, under “Move to library…”, is “Copy to library…” (it is also under the “⋯” at the end of the row of buttons on the details' “Info” tab).

The dialog is titled “Copy to library”. Under the paper's title are the libraries it can be copied to, here only “根毛发育” (30 papers), and a line saying that its PDFs, annotations, reading position and conversations about it are copied over, where its screening starts as pending, and that the paper here stays as it is. Click “Copy”.

The window does not move: it stays in “植物单细胞转录组”, the paper is still in the list, and the library still holds 40 papers. The top of the window says “Copied to “根毛发育”.”, with “Open” beside it to go and look. “根毛发育” now holds 31 papers, and the newcomer's screening is “Pending”: whether it belongs is judged again, for the root hair topic.

TipAfter copying, the two are separate: an annotation added or a note changed on one side does not reach the other. When a paper belongs only to the new topic and the old library has no use for it, use “Move to library…”: the paper goes over with its PDFs and annotations, and the original stays in this library's trash. Since v0.22.0 the window stays where it is after a move too, so you can move the next paper right away.

8Already there: append or overwrite

Next you come across another paper about root hair cells: a multi-organ single-cell atlas of oak (Hui et al., 2026, PLoS genetics), in which QrIAA14 is enriched in root hair cells, the cells next to them and the root cap. Tag it and the buckwheat paper “根毛” (root hair), click that tag in the sidebar, and the list shows these two. Click the first, hold ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) and click the second to select both; right-click, and the last group of the menu reads “Copy 2 papers to library…”.

Click it and choose “根毛发育”: a line in a warning colour appears in the dialog, saying that “根毛发育” already has 2 of them, with their titles. The buckwheat paper is the one copied in the last section; the oak paper was also fetched by “根毛发育”'s own search, so each library has a record of it. Two lines below say what each way does, and the buttons become “Cancel”, “Append” and “Overwrite”:

  • Append: everything already there stays; the tags, notes, PDFs and annotations from here are added, and empty details filled in.
  • Overwrite: wherever both have something, this side wins, including the title and other details, notes, tags, reading state and annotations; what only that side has stays, and its screening is unchanged.

Neither adds a paper to “根毛发育”. Here only the tag should go over, and nothing of the records there needs changing, so click “Append”. The default button is “Overwrite”, which Enter presses; appending takes a click. The top of the window says ““根毛发育” already had these 2 papers: their files and annotations were added to them.” Click “Open” beside it to go to “根毛发育”, and click “根毛” in the sidebar:

The library still holds 31 papers; nothing was added. From here each library judges for itself whether to include them: a paper included for the single-cell topic may be left out for the root hair one.

Recap

  • While a library screens, extracts, finds full texts or translates, leave it and work elsewhere: it goes on in the background, and its results go into its own library.
  • The home page's card and the bottom of another library's sidebar say how far it is, and a click goes back; when it is done, the top of the window says so.
  • A question across libraries is asked on the home page: click “Ask my libraries”, or just say “look in my libraries for …”.
  • The source cards of the answer name each paper's library, and a click opens that paper there; the other libraries are read-only in that conversation.
  • A paper both topics can use: right-click, “Copy to library…”. The original stays as it is, the other library gets a copy whose screening starts at “Pending”, and from then on each side changes its own.
  • Where the other library already has the same paper, the dialog asks first: “Append” keeps what is there and adds what is here, “Overwrite” lets this side win wherever both have something; neither adds a paper.
  • Next: write the screening suggestions in, see “Confirm the screening decisions” in Start from a topic.

About this tutorial. The screenshots come from one real run in November 2026 on macOS with v0.19.0, with the English interface; the Chinese page shows a run of its own with the Chinese interface, whose numbers differ. Screening used GPT-6.1 Sol and the question Claude Sonnet 5.5, both through a local gateway; the 30 root hair papers were fetched from PubMed that day. Sections 7 and 8 come from another run, on November 28, 2026 with v0.22.0 and the English interface: both libraries were fetched anew from PubMed, 40 single-cell studies of plant roots and 30 root hair studies, so the lists differ from the earlier sections; no model was used in them. A model judges and answers differently each time, so numbers and wording will differ when you follow along; the steps are the same.

The assistant can be wrong. Its suggestions and answers need your confirmation; check key numbers and conclusions against the original.
