Which to read first: impact factor, zone and warnings

You have collected dozens of papers and cannot read them all, so you need to pick a few to start with. A journal's impact factor and zone are a rough but common guide; other journals are on a warning list and deserve a second look before you cite them. In this tutorial you sort the paper list by these two values, look at all the metrics of the journal a paper appeared in, switch to another zone table, spot warning journals, and finally have the assistant and the command line look them up.

Scenario
The library “植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics) holds 28 included papers, and you must decide which to read first and which to treat with care.
Needs
A library whose papers have journal names; to ask the assistant, a model provider set up in “Settings › AI”. Looking up zones needs no network and no model.
Time
About 5 minutes.

1The two columns in the list

Open the library. To the right of “Journal”, the paper list has two columns, “Impact factor” and “Zone”. The numbers are not looked up online: the app carries a journal table that merges four lists:

  • JCR 2025: the impact factor and the JCR zone (Q1–Q4).
  • CAS journal zone table, upgraded edition 2025: the broad-category zone (zones 1–4) and whether the journal is Top.
  • XR (emerging journal) zone table 2026: the zone and whether the journal is Top.
  • International journal warning list 2025: the journals on the list and the reasons.

Each paper is looked up in this table by the journal name in its record. Full names and PubMed-style abbreviations (for example Nat Commun) are both recognized, and so is PubMed's way of adding the place of publication in brackets after the journal name, for example Advanced science (Weinheim, Baden-Wurttemberg, Germany). So no network is used, and your papers are not sent to anyone.

If you cannot see the two columns, right-click the table header and tick “Impact factor” and “Zone”; untick the columns you do not need in the same menu.

2Sort by impact factor

Click the column name “Impact factor” and the list sorts from high to low; click again for low to high. Clicking “Zone” starts from zone 1, with Top journals first within the same zone.

Papers whose journal is not in the table show “—” and sink to the end, whichever way you sort. Scroll to the end of the list in this example:

TipThe impact factor and the zone describe the journal, not this paper. They are an average or a rank of how often all articles of the journal's previous two years were cited, and the citations of individual papers within one journal differ a great deal; numbers from different fields cannot be compared directly either. Using them to order what to read first is fine; to judge whether a paper is well done, you still have to read it.

3The journal metrics of one paper

Open a paper. On the “Info” tab on the right, below the line with the journal and year, there is one more line with all the metrics of this journal. The “Zone” column of the list has room for only one kind of zone; here all four are written out:

Rest the pointer on this line and it lists the subjects of this journal in each table. In this example: “JCR Q1: MULTIDISCIPLINARY SCIENCES”, “CAS category: multidisciplinary journals” and “XR category: multidisciplinary journals”, and last the version of the data. When the journal name in the record differs from the one in the table, the first line says which name it was matched by: for the Advanced science (Weinheim, Baden-Wurttemberg, Germany) paper the hover text says “Matched as “Advanced Science””, so you can check that it is the same journal.

While the details are open the list is narrower, and columns that do not fit are put away; the header shows “+3 columns”. In this example the “Journal”, “Zone” and “Screening” columns are put away, and “Impact factor” is still there.

4Switch to another zone table

Which zone the “Zone” column shows is chosen in “Settings › Appearance › Journal zones”: “CAS”, “XR” or “JCR”. The default is CAS. In this example, switch to JCR.

Click “Back” to return to the list, and the “Zone” column now shows Q1, Q2. Click the column name again to sort by it:

The two kinds of zone differ a lot. International journal of molecular sciences is zone 3 in CAS and Q1 in JCR; Frontiers in plant science and Plant methods are zone 2 in CAS and also Q1 in JCR. The JCR zone takes the best one among the subjects the journal belongs to; which one to use depends on which your institution or the journal you submit to recognizes. When you are done, click back to “CAS” in the same place; the screenshots after this use CAS zones.

5Journals on the warning list

None of the original 28 papers in this library is in a warning journal. To see what one looks like, click “Add papers”, choose “Add by DOI / PMID / web address…”, and add an article from Natural Resources Forum (DOI 10.1111/1477-8947.12555, about foreign investment in agriculture and methane emissions in sub-Saharan Africa, unrelated to the topic of this library). This journal is on the international journal warning list 2025.

Once it is added, the impact factor and zone in its row are no longer gray but in a warning color. Click the column name “Zone” twice to sort from zone 4, and it comes first. Rest the pointer on the number and you see the journal name, all its metrics and the reason for the warning:

In its details, the line under the journal reads “IF 4.7 · JCR Q2 · CAS Zone 4 · XR Zone 3”, followed, in the same color, by “International journal warning list 2025: paper mill”.

Being on the warning list does not mean this particular paper has a problem; the list speaks of the risk of the journal as a whole. For a paper like this, check its data and methods more closely before you cite it, or find a paper published elsewhere that backs it up.

TipIn the tables the app carries, the international journal warning list 2025 has only 5 journals; journals marked “Under Review” in the XR journal zone table are also counted as warnings, with the reason “XR journal zone table Under Review”, for example Heliyon.

6Filter by range: year, impact factor, zone

Sorting puts what you want first; filtering keeps only what you want. Among the row of filter buttons above the list, “Year”, “Impact factor” and “Zone” can filter by range. The screenshots in this section come from another demo library of the same name on v0.17.0, with 61 papers from 2021 to 2026, imported from a PubMed search, so the numbers differ from the sections above.

Click “Year”: at the top is “All”, then “Last 3 years”, “Last 5 years”, “Last 10 years” and “Custom range…”, and below those one row per year. Beside each item is the number of papers in the whole library that match it. Here “Last 3 years” is 45 papers, which is the 15, 5 and 25 papers of 2024, 2025 and 2026.

“Custom range…” opens a small dialog with two boxes for the earliest and the latest year. Filling in only one box works too: only the first means that year and later, only the second means that year and earlier. Papers without a year are not in the result.

“Impact factor” has “≥ 3”, “≥ 5”, “≥ 10”, “≥ 20” and “Custom range…”, again with the number of papers for each. The custom dialog also has two boxes, lowest and highest. The impact factor comes from the JCR data that comes with the app, and papers whose journal is not in it (a preprint on bioRxiv, say) are not in the result.

“Zone” gives three steps according to the zone table in use: with CAS zones they are “CAS Zone 1”, “CAS Zones 1–2” and “CAS Zones 1–3”, and after switching to JCR they are “JCR Q1”, “JCR Q1–Q2” and “JCR Q1–Q3”. The lower half of the menu is “Partition: CAS”, “Partition: XR” and “Partition: JCR”; you can switch tables right here, and the “Zone” column of the list follows.

Several can be combined. In this example “Last 3 years”, “≥ 10” and “CAS Zone 1” were chosen in turn: the three buttons turn green, reading “Year: Last 3 years”, “Impact factor: ≥ 10” and “Zone: CAS Zone 1”, and “Clear filters” appears on the right; the bottom of the list reads “Showing 20 / 61 papers”, and the numbers beside “All papers”, “Unread” and “Missing PDF” in the sidebar are also the filtered 20.

7Ask the assistant, or use the command line

The assistant can look up this table too: it has a tool called “Look up journal zones and impact factor”. Click “Assistant” in the sidebar and ask:

Ask

Which papers in this library are published in CAS zone 1 journals? What is each about?

The model used in this example is claude/claude-sonnet-5-5, and it answered within half a minute. It first looked up journals 13 times, then listed the papers by journal, one or two sentences for each:

What it lists below is 6 journals: Horticulture Research 2 papers, Advanced Science 1, New Phytologist 1, Genome Biology 4, Nature Communications 3 and Communications Biology 1, 12 papers in all, which agrees with what the list shows when sorted by zone. But its opening says “7 papers” and “5 journals”. After listing them it noticed this itself: it first changed it to “11 papers”, then wrote “I miscounted. Above, 12 papers are actually listed”. The last number is right, but the opening sentence stayed in the answer. Count such numbers yourself, or check them in the list sorted by zone.

The “Notes” at the end of the answer also list the zones of the remaining journals. It points out that IJMS and other journals are Q1 in JCR but not zone 1 in CAS, that the bioRxiv paper is a preprint and has no zone, and that the Natural Resources Forum article is unrelated to the topic and its journal is on the warning list, and suggests checking whether it should be included.

You can also look up without opening the window. wisp journal_metrics takes a journal name (full name, PubMed-style abbreviation or ISSN) or the ID of a paper, and returns JSON:

Recap

  • The “Impact factor” and “Zone” columns of the paper list come from four lists that come with the app, with no network; right-click the header to show or put away columns.
  • Click a column name to sort and bring the papers to read first to the top; journals that cannot be found (only bioRxiv in this example) show “—” and sink to the end.
  • In the details, the line under the journal gives all the metrics; “Settings › Appearance › Journal zones” decides whether the list shows CAS, XR or JCR.
  • Journals on the warning list are shown in a warning color; hover to see the reason.
  • “Year”, “Impact factor” and “Zone” on the filter bar filter by range: last N years or a custom range, ≥ some impact factor or a custom range, zone 1 up to zones 1–3, and several can be combined.
  • The assistant and wisp journal_metrics both look up the same table; check the assistant's counts yourself. Once you have picked the papers to read, continue with Read a batch of papers into one table.
