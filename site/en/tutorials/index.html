Tutorials · Eighteen research scenarios

Start from one concrete thing

Each tutorial is the record of a real run: a research topic you have just taken over, a paper to read closely, a manuscript your advisor has marked up and that has to go back. Apart from 02, which starts from a blank page, they all use the same library, “植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics). Read them from start to end, or read only the one you need right now.

Which to read first
No papers yet: start with 01. Only an idea or a manuscript, and no clear topic: start with 02. Papers already in Zotero or in a folder: start with 03. Just one PDF that you want to read first: see 04. After that, the tutorials do not depend on each other.
Which need a model
All except 03 and 04 use the assistant (11 only in the last step), so first set up a model provider of your own in “Settings › AI”. The first step of 01 explains how. The voices in 08 also need a DeepSeek API key, and so do text recognition in 03 and web search in 10.
Version
Written for v0.10.0 to v0.22.0, run on macOS. On Windows, read Ctrl for ⌘; everything else is the same.

ICollect

01

Start from a topic: search, import, screen

You have just taken over a research direction and have no papers. Write down the topic, let the assistant draft search queries, import in bulk from PubMed and Europe PMC, then have the assistant read each paper while you decide whether to include it.

Needs
A model provider
Time
About 15 minutes
 02

Talk first, build the library later: starting from the assistant on the home page

You have only an idea, or a half-written proposal. Skip the library and talk to the assistant on the home page: have it search, take in a few papers, or read every entry of the reference list of a Word manuscript. When the picture is clear, make it a library with one click.

Needs
A model provider
Time
About 10 minutes
 03

Bring in the papers you already have: Zotero, PDFs and records

Your papers are in Zotero, in a folder of PDFs collected over years, or in a .bib file your advisor sent. Bring them into one library with their tags, notes and PDFs, complete the bibliographic records, and find the missing full texts. Shrink a PDF that is too heavy, recognise the text of a scan, and file a supplement that came in on its own under its paper.

Needs
No model
Time
About 10 minutes

IIRead

04

Just one PDF to read: drop it in and read

A colleague sent you a PDF and you want to read it right away and mark a few lines. Drop it on the home page and it opens in reading mode, with your annotations and your place remembered. When you are done, make it a library, or move it to an existing one; to send it on, export the PDF with your marks.

Needs
A PDF, no model
Time
About 5 minutes
 05

Read one paper closely: read, mark, ask, note

One paper you need to understand thoroughly. Read it next to its translation, highlight and underline, ask the assistant about the passage you do not follow, save the answer as a note, and capture the key figures and tables into Figures. Click a figure number in the text to see the figure, and write a text box on the page. Drag a split wider on one side, and read original, translation and original in three columns. Get around a long paper with the scrollbar, the page overview and two pages side by side.

Needs
A paper with a PDF, a model provider
Time
About 15 minutes
 06

Turn a paper into a mind map

You have read a long paper and its structure is still loose. Open a map in a column beside the PDF, have the model read the full text and draft it, click a page number on a node to get back to that sentence, add sentences you read as nodes, write down what to ask at the lab meeting, and copy it all as a Markdown outline.

Needs
A paper with a PDF; a model provider for the draft
Time
About 10 minutes
 07

Read several papers side by side

Two or three papers on the same question with different methods, read side by side. Each brings its own translation, you ask the assistant about them together, and the sources jump back to each original. Open one in its own window, move back and forth with Back and Forward, and find the conversation again later in History.

Needs
Two or three papers with PDFs, a model provider
Time
About 15 minutes
 08

Listen to a paper: read aloud and podcast

Read with your ears when your eyes are busy. It reads aloud from the current page to the references and marks the sentence being read. Or two hosts can turn the whole paper into a podcast that keeps playing when you leave the paper, with the saved audio and script ready to play again any time.

Needs
A paper with a PDF, a DeepSeek API key, a model provider
Time
About 10 minutes, plus listening time
 09

Turn a paper into lab-meeting slides

You present this paper at next week's lab meeting. Capture the figures you will talk about from the PDF into Figures, then use /slides to have the assistant read the full text and write a PowerPoint file with the figures, the page numbers of the sources, and the speaker script in the notes, also after your lab's own template.

Needs
A paper with a PDF, a model provider
Time
About 10 minutes
 10

Ask the whole library, and check the answers

“Which sample preparation methods has this field used, and what are their pros and cons?” Have the assistant read the whole library to answer, with a source for every conclusion. Then check, one by one, that the sentences it quotes are in the original. For what the library does not hold, let it search the web; ask for a diagram and it draws one in the answer. Before asking, find the exact words in full texts and marks, and call your own skills with /.

Needs
A library of a few dozen papers, a model provider
Time
About 10 minutes

IIIOrganize

11

Which to read first: impact factor, zone and warnings

Rank the papers to read first by the “Impact factor” and “Zone” columns of the list, filter by ranges of year, impact factor and zone, see all the metrics of one journal in the details, switch to JCR or the New Sharp zones, and recognize the journals on the warning list. The data ships with the app and needs no network.

Needs
A library; a model provider if you ask the assistant
Time
About 5 minutes
 12

Read a batch of papers into one table

Species, organ, protoplasts or nuclei, sequencing platform: define your own custom fields and have the assistant find the values in the original text, paper by paper, with the sentence it relied on. You confirm and write them in, then export them as a table.

Needs
A library, a model provider
Time
About 15 minutes

IVWrite

13

Write a review in stages

Start with /review: the writing brief, a first framework, the outline, and section-by-section writing, with you confirming each of the four steps. Every citation leaves a piece of evidence, and you judge whether the source supports the claim.

Needs
A screened library, ideally with full texts; a model provider
Time
About 40 minutes
 14

Write a paper: candidate sentences, citations and sources

While you write the introduction, the library is at your right hand: find sentences, add citations to a paragraph you have written, and check which original sentence each citation rests on. Or give the assistant one instruction and let it write straight into the manuscript. Put figures from “Materials” into the manuscript, and they export with it; a table written in the manuscript becomes a real table in Word.

Needs
A few papers with full text, a model provider
Time
About 20 minutes
 15

Writing directly, or writing in Wisp Study

One introduction paragraph written twice: once by asking a chat model directly, once on the Writing page with the assistant working from the library. Every reference is looked up on Crossref and checked against its abstract, to see what each way gives you and what it leaves you to check.

Needs
A library, a model provider
Time
About 20 minutes
 16

Revise with co-authors: Word comments and tracked changes

Your advisor left a string of comments in Word. Import the manuscript, deal with them one by one, have the assistant review it once more, and finally export a Word file with tracked changes to send back, with the layout untouched.

Needs
A .docx manuscript, a model provider
Time
About 15 minutes

VFollow up

17

Keep up with new papers, and hand the library to other tools

Let a saved search query run by itself every week, and have the assistant go through the new papers first. Then use wisp and MCP to hand the same library to scripts in the terminal and to outside AI clients.

Needs
A library with a search query; some comfort with the terminal
Time
About 15 minutes
 18

Several topics at once: work in the background, ask across libraries

Two topics, two libraries: one library has the assistant screen in the background while you work in the other, and the window says when it is done; then ask the home page a question across both, with sources that name their library; and copy a paper both can use from one to the other.

Needs
Two libraries, a model provider
Time
About 15 minutes

About these tutorials. The screenshots and numbers come from a real run on macOS in November 2026. The model answers differently each time and the databases change every day, so the wording and numbers you see will differ a little. The steps are the same. The screenshots show the Chinese interface; the text uses the labels of the English one (Settings › General › Interface language).

The assistant can be wrong. Its suggestions and answers need your confirmation; check key numbers and conclusions against the original.
