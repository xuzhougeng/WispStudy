Tutorial 15 · Follow up

Keep up with new papers, and hand the library to other tools

The library is built and has been screened once. Now it has to move with the field without costing you half a day every week. This tutorial first sets up search queries that run on a schedule and lets the assistant read new papers first, in the window; then it uses the wisp command line and MCP to hand the same library to the terminal and to outside AI clients.

Scenario
“植物单细胞转录组” (plant single-cell transcriptomics) already holds 29 screened papers. You want to follow new single-nucleus (snRNA-seq) work too, and to query the library from a terminal and from Claude Code.
Needs
A library with saved search queries and a model provider (for screening); you should be comfortable with a terminal.
Time
About 15 minutes.

1Add a search query and preview it first

The library already has two search queries: PubMed for atlases and methods, and Europe PMC for stress responses. Papers on single-nucleus transcriptomes often fall outside both, so you add a third that looks only at titles containing single-nucleus.

Open “Search and import” on the left, click “New search”, fill in “Source”, “Query”, “Name” and “Retrieve” (how many of the newest papers to take, 50 by default, 2000 at most), and click “Save”. Saving does not run it. This example uses the command line, with the same effect (the command line is covered in section 4):

The new query appears at the bottom of the page, marked “Never run”. First click its “Preview search”: this step only asks the database for results and writes nothing to the library.

Look down the list. The few papers already in the library are grayed out, with “In library · Included” on the right, which shows that this query agrees with the direction of the library; the new ones are all plant single-nucleus transcriptome studies, so it is fine to run.

TipThe query is sent to the database you chose (Europe PMC here). A broad query that matches thousands of papers brings back only the newest few each time, so split it into several focused ones.

2Run, import history and automatic updates

Click “Run and import”. This time it retrieves 16 papers, adds 12 and skips the 4 already there: Wisp Study recognizes papers already in the library by DOI, PMID and title, and those in the trash count too. Run it once more right away and the result is “retrieved 16, added 0, skipped 16 existing”. The first run fetches as many papers as “Retrieve” says; later runs go only for papers the library does not have yet, and usually end after one or two requests.

Every run leaves a row in “Import history” at the bottom of the page. A row that added papers has “Undo” on its right: it removes the papers that batch added and that nobody has handled yet; papers you have screened, read, starred, written notes on, or given tags or attachments are kept. You are asked once more before it is undone.

“Automatic updates” at the top of the page is “Off” by default, and then the database is contacted only when you click “Run and import”. This example chose “Weekly”, and the note beside it changed to say: enabled searches run at this interval while the app is open, and runs missed while it was closed are caught up the next time you open it. A query whose run fails is tried again after an hour at most. Click “Disable” on a card to keep that one out of automatic updates and “Run all”.

When an automatic update brings in new papers and that library is open, the window says “Automatic update: N new papers awaiting screening”. New papers are always “Pending”, and the number next to “Screening” on the left is how many are waiting for you.

3The weekly screening: only what is new

How to screen a few hundred papers the first time is in Tutorial 01. After that the weekly job is much smaller: open “Screening”, leave the “Screening criteria” above as they are, and click “Ask assistant to screen 12 papers”. The assistant reads several papers at once, the full text where there is one and the abstract where there is not. This round was read in under a minute, with the suggestions Included 10 and Related 2.

The Screening page now offers three ways to look. “Batch” is the queue in the figure above; “One by one” shows one paper at a time, with this round's papers on the left and, on the right, the assistant's suggestion, its reason, the sentence it quoted and the abstract, with four decision buttons below; you can also press Y / R / U / N, and A accepts the suggestion. The two papers suggested as “Related” are worth a look one by one:

The two papers are GAMETE maps the genetic architecture of chromatin accessibility in rice pollen at single-nucleus resolution and a 2017 single-nucleus sequencing study of maize haploid induction (whole-genome sequencing, not a transcriptome). The assistant read only the abstracts, the sentences it quoted can be found in them, and the judgments hold up.

The third way is the “Confirm suggestions” page: click “Confirm 12 suggestions” to enter it (the switch at the top of the page has only “Batch” and “One by one”). Each paper has a row saying the decision that will be written; change a row you disagree with, untick those you are unsure of, and click “Confirm 12 papers” to write them all at once. Nothing changes before you write, and after writing you can still “Undo”.

This example writes them as suggested, and the notice reads “Confirmed 12 papers: Included 10 · Related 2.” The screening decision is yours from start to finish; the assistant only suggests.

After you change the screening criteria Existing suggestions are marked “Suggestion under old criteria”, and the button becomes “Screen N under current criteria”. For papers already screened you can click “Review N screened papers” to have the assistant read them again under the new criteria: those where it disagrees with you go back to the queue, grouped under “Disagrees with your decision”, and you choose whether to keep or change; your original decisions are not touched.

4Use wisp in a terminal

wisp is the entry point that opens no window, and it comes with the installer: on macOS it is at /Applications/Wisp Study.app/Contents/MacOS/wisp; on Windows it sits next to the main program WispStudy.exe, and for the installed version in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Wisp Study; on Linux it is also next to the main program. It reads and writes the same data folder as the app settings, and can be used while the app is open; --data-dir FOLDER names a different one. The output is always JSON; on an error the message goes to stderr and the exit code is 1. Below it is shortened to wisp; wisp help lists all commands and wisp COMMAND --help lists the parameters of one command. With only one library you can leave out -l.

Note that the screening decision is stored under an English key: excluded is shown in the interface as “Related”, and the lookup table is in labels (in the Chinese interface the value is 相关).

search_fulltext searches only papers whose full text is saved: here 22 papers have text and 19 do not (the 12 just imported have no PDF yet). read_fulltext reads at most 8 pages at a time, to check the context.

search_online writes nothing, like “Preview search” in the window, and for a paper already in the library inLibrary gives its ID and screening decision. The batch ID that run_search returns can be given to undo_import to undo the whole batch, with the same rules as in the window.

5Scheduled updates while the window is closed

Automatic updates run only while the app is open. If you do not open the window often, have the system's scheduler run wisp sync_searches regularly: it runs only the search queries that are due, and it decides what is “due” by the same interval you chose in section 2, so a scheduler that runs more often does not search twice. On macOS and Linux add a line with crontab -e to look once an hour:

launchd on macOS and Task Scheduler on Windows can do the same: run this command on a schedule.

Notesync_searches handles only libraries whose automatic updates are not off. When automatic updates are “Off” it does nothing and returns {"runs": [], "added": 0, "failed": 0}; with --force it ignores the interval and the switch and runs every enabled search query. After a partial failure the exit code may still be 0, so look at failed and runs[].error in the JSON.

Where writing stops

Without --read-only there are 18 more tools, and all of them change data: creating a library; importing PDFs, BibTeX and Zotero; saving, running, syncing and deleting search queries; undoing an import; finding and downloading PDFs; organizing tags and reading status; writing a section and the outline of a manuscript; exporting and saving notes; and also suggest_screening and suggest_field.

  • For the screening decision and custom fields, an outside client can at most make suggestions (these two tools are offered only without --read-only): suggest_screening proposes a screening suggestion and suggest_field proposes a field value, both with a verbatim quotation. They show up on the “Screening” and “Extract” pages waiting for you to confirm, and no tool can set a screening decision or a field directly.
  • The assistant in the window asks you first before it imports, runs or deletes search queries, undoes an import, finds PDFs, organizes papers or revises a manuscript; an outside client calling wisp mcp has no such step, and once it calls, the thing is done.
NoteIf you only want an outside client to look things up and read, use --read-only. Reading full text can still leave a text cache in the library, but it does not change papers or decisions.

Recap

  • You added a single-nucleus transcriptome search query, previewed it and ran it, which added 12 papers; running it again added 0, and the import history can undo a whole batch.
  • Automatic updates are set to weekly; while the window is closed, cron runs wisp sync_searches, which runs only the queries that are due.
  • The assistant read the 12 new papers in under a minute; after you looked at the two “Related” ones one by one, you wrote everything at once on the “Confirm suggestions” page.
  • The same library is queried with wisp in a terminal and read with wisp mcp --read-only in an outside client; the screening decision is always yours, made in the window.
  • Once new papers are in, go back to Ask the whole library, and check the answers or Read a batch of papers into one table.
