Tutorial 09 · Read

Turn a paper into lab-meeting slides

You have read the paper, and next week you present it at the lab meeting. This tutorial first cuts the figures you want to show from the PDF and saves them to the figure library, then has the assistant read the full text and write a PowerPoint file: a title slide, a few points per slide, figures with captions, the source page number in the footer, and the speaker notes in the notes. The layout is a starting point, not a finished deck; take it to PowerPoint, Keynote or WPS and keep editing. The last section gives the assistant an earlier deck of your group as the reference, so the new one takes its master, layouts and colours.

Scenario
Turn a paper on a single-cell atlas of rice callus into a lab-meeting talk.
Needs
A paper with a PDF, and a model provider set up in “Settings › AI”; to make slides after your own deck, a .pptx as well.
Time
About 10 minutes, of which the assistant takes about two minutes to write the slides.

Contents

1Cut the figures you want to show

The paper to present is A single-cell transcriptome atlas reveals the transcriptionally earliest state in rice callus, BMC Plant Biology, 2026, 15 pages in full, with its PDF already stored in the library. Search for callus above the list, click this paper and switch to “PDF” on the right.

Figures in the slides come only from the figure library, and the assistant does not crop figures from the PDF by itself. So decide first which ones to show: this paper is about which cells appear first in callus, so it needs Fig. 1 (the cell atlas), Fig. 2 (the developmental trajectory) and Fig. 4 (the establishment of the root meristem niche). Turn to page 5 and click “Screenshot” on the toolbar (the button becomes “Cancel screenshot”), then select Fig. 1 on the page. After you select it, four buttons appear beside it: “Save to figures”, “Ask about screenshot”, “Mark” and “Cancel”.

When you have not decided whether to present a figure, click “Mark”: the area stays on the page as a dashed frame. Next time you click into the frame you can likewise save it or ask about it, or delete it. Circle every figure you might present while you read, and save them one by one before you make the slides, so that none is missed.

2Save them to the figure library

Click “Save to figures”. In the dialog, the screenshot is on the left with “Page 5” and its source below it; on the right are the figure label, caption, tags and notes. The caption of this page sits right under the figure, and both the label Fig. 1 and the whole caption were read from the page text. The top of the dialog reminds you to “Check and correct them before saving”.

The tags are empty. Click the AI icon to the right of “Tags”, and the model looked at the figure and filled in UMAP, 点图, 多面板组合排版 (UMAP, dot plot, multi-panel layout). These tags are used later to find figures in the figure library. You can edit them or leave them out. The AI icon beside a field only makes a suggestion; nothing is stored until you click “Save”.

Figure 2 was retaken on v0.17.0, with the tags not yet filled in. While the dialog is open, the page behind it is no longer dimmed and you can still select text on it and copy it into the dialog. Hold the dialog's title or its image to drag it aside. After saving, the figure on the page is left with a green dashed frame, with a check mark and the figure label at its top left, so wherever you turn you can tell which figures have been saved. Click that label to edit the caption and tags, or to “Remove” it. For how it looks, see Figures 8 and 9 of Read one paper closely.

Save Fig. 2 on page 7 and Fig. 4 on page 9 in the same way. Switch to “Figures” under “Favorites” on the left and all three are there. Above them you can filter by tag (“UMAP 1”, “multi-panel layout 1”, “dot plot 1”), and the search box looks through captions, figure labels, tags and notes. Click a card to open it and edit its fields again; “Open source” goes back to the page the screenshot came from, and “Copy image” and “Save as…” take it elsewhere.

3Have the assistant write slides with /slides

Return to this paper and click “Ask”. Type / in the box, choose /slides from the list of commands, and write what you need after it:

Ask

/slides For next week's lab meeting, about 8 slides, explaining clearly how the earliest transcriptional state in callus was found

When you ask about a single paper, you do not need to say which paper after /slides. In the “Assistant” of the library, write /slides P3, or just say “turn this paper into a PPT for the lab meeting”. The assistant first reads the full text, then looks up the figures already saved for this paper in the figure library, and the conversation folds this into one line, “Read paper · Query figures”.

Before the file is written, a save dialog opens, defaulting to “Documents” with the file name taken from the title of the slides. Closing the dialog means nothing is written, and an existing file of the same name is not overwritten. In this example it took under two minutes and wrote 水稻愈伤组织最早转录状态的单细胞图谱.pptx (the file name is “single-cell atlas of the earliest transcriptional state in rice callus”).

The answer does not repeat the contents of the slides but tells you what it did: a title slide plus 9 content slides, arranged as “Background and question → Methods → Main results → Conclusions and limits”; which slides carry Fig. 1, Fig. 2 and Fig. 4 from the figure library; and, because the captions are long, only the first three lines are kept on the slide, with the full text in the notes. It also says what it could not do: Fig. 3, Fig. 5 and Fig. 6 are not in the figure library, so the slides on subcluster analysis and on differentiating callus have no figure. “If you like, select and save these figures on the PDF pages and I can do it again.” Finally it points out two things to make clear when you present: the “earliest state” is inferred from RNA velocity and pseudotime, with no lineage tracing; and the link between OsDWT1 and gibberellin is only a coordinated change in expression, with no genetic validation.

TipIf you want a figure in the slides, save it to the figure library first and then say /slides again. Save tables as figures too: the assistant does not draw tables or charts by itself.

4Open the file and keep editing

Double-click the .pptx to open it in PowerPoint, Keynote or WPS. Below are six of the slides. The title slide shows the authors, year and journal. On a slide with a figure, the points are on the left and the figure and its caption on the right or below. The footer of each slide gives the source and page number, for example “Lyu et al., 2026 · p. 5 (Fig. 1)”, so if you are asked about a sentence, you can turn back to that page of the original.

The speaker notes are in the notes of each slide, and slides with a figure also carry its full caption. For example, the notes of the Fig. 1 slide begin with a sentence to say, “Point out three kinds of evidence for the founder cells: homologs of Arabidopsis early callus initiation cell genes, homologs of WOX11, and in situ hybridization of RAmy3D/E”, followed by the full caption of Fig. 1. You see it at a glance in presenter view.

Without a reference, the layout is arranged by the program: white background, one accent color, no animation. To use your group's template, see the next section; adjusting the wording and splitting one slide into two are done in the presentation software. For a bigger change in structure, keep talking in the same conversation (“squeeze the methods into one slide and merge results 3 and 4”) and the assistant writes a new file and leaves the old one alone.

NoteThe points are a summary the model wrote after reading the full text, so go through the numbers and conclusions against the original; the page numbers in the footers are there for that. When slides are generated, the full text of this paper and the label, caption and tags of each figure in the figure library are sent to the model provider you set up. The images themselves are placed in the file from this machine by the program and do not go through the model. A figure is sent out only if you clicked the AI icon when filling in its tags or caption.

5Make slides after your own deck

A group's talks usually look alike: one master, a layout each for the cover, the section slides and the content slides, the group's name in the footer. Give the assistant a .pptx you presented before as the reference, and the new slides are built on its master and layouts. The reference here is a lab-meeting talk on a single-cell atlas of the Arabidopsis root tip, lab-template-root-atlas.pptx, seven slides: a cover, two section slides (“1. Background”, “2. Results”) and four content slides, with a green band along the bottom, dark green titles, and “Plant Development Group · Lab meeting” and the slide number in the footer.

This time the paper is another one: the single-nucleus atlas of the maize ligular region published in Nature Communications in 2026, A single-nucleus atlas of ligular region uncovers brassinosteroid-driven cell fate transitions controlling leaf angle in maize. Its Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 were already saved to the figure library as in sections 1 and 2.

Go to “Assistant” in the library's sidebar and drop the reference .pptx on the input box. The last line of the “Write” column on the start page says just this: “Make slides after a deck of your own: pick a .pptx file, or drop it here.” The file sits above the input box as a small chip, and the × on the chip takes it off again. Then write what you want:

Assistant

Use this deck as the reference and turn the maize ligular-region single-nucleus atlas paper into a lab-meeting talk. Please answer in English.

The last sentence of the request is there because the first try, without it, got its answer in Chinese (the slides themselves were in English). From sending to the finished file took under a minute; the file is A single-nucleus atlas of the maize ligular region- brassinosteroid-driven cell .pptx, its name taken from the title of the deck, and it is saved as in section 3.

The answer says what it took from the reference: “It uses your reference deck's master, layouts and structure: cover, "1. Background", "2. Results", then a closing discussion slide. Each slide has 3–5 bullets, as in the template.” A list of the 11 slides follows. It also says what it did not copy, and what to check: the captions of Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 were too long, so only their first three lines are on the slides and the full text is in the notes; the last bullet of the discussion slide is its own placeholder, “loosely modelled on your template's "Next: pilot batch" line”; and the “about 64% of 261 BR-induced genes” on slide 9 should be checked against page 7 of the paper. Only Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 are in the figure library, so the other results slides have no figure.

Opened in LibreOffice, here are six of the 11 slides; compare them with Figure 7. The green band, the trees and the rabbits are drawn on the reference's layouts and are all there. The cover uses the reference's cover layout, the two section slides “1. Background” and “2. Results” its section layout, and the rest its content layout, with the same dark green titles. The source in the footer (such as “Wang et al., 2026 · Fig. 1 · p. 3”) and the slide number stand where the reference has its footer and its number. On a slide with a figure, the program still places the figure and its caption beside the points, as in section 4.

Two things are not carried over. None of the reference's own seven slides goes into the new file, and so neither does text written on those slides (the footer “Plant Development Group · Lab meeting”, for one); the new footers give the source. The reference file itself is not changed. Ask for slides again in the same conversation and the same reference is used.

TipA reference works best when it is built on layouts: titles and points in the layout's title and body places, a section slide holding only a title. An earlier try here used a reference whose section slides had a line of small text under the title; the assistant wrote such a line on its section slides too, and in the new file those two slides fell on the content layout. If most slides of the reference are text boxes drawn on blank slides, the new slides can only take its background and theme, the slides will not be laid out like it, and the assistant says so in its answer (the reference here uses layouts, so this did not come up). An old .ppt cannot be read; save it as .pptx first.

Recap

  • Use “Screenshot” on the PDF to select the figure you want to show and “Save to figures”; for the ones you have not decided on, “Mark” them as a dashed frame.
  • The figure label and caption are read from the page, the model can fill in tags from the figure, and you look them over before saving.
  • /slides has the assistant read the full text and look up the figure library, and write a .pptx with figures, source footers and speaker notes in the notes; you choose where to save it.
  • A figure missing from the slides is one you have not saved: save it to the figure library and run it again. Check numbers and conclusions against the original.
  • Drop an earlier .pptx of your group on “Assistant” with the request, and the new slides are built on its master and layouts, the footer and number in its places; its own slides are not carried over and the file is not changed.
  • Next: widen the question to the whole library; see Ask the whole library, and check the answers.
